# Context: Steps in a Successful AI Strategy

Reference links: Google Cloud & OnStrategy AI Strategy

## Step 1: Establish the company-wide vision

A successful AI strategy begins with aligning AI initiatives to the organization's broader business objectives. This requires a collaborative operating model where business and technology teams work closely together rather than pursuing isolated AI projects.

- **Integrated Approach:** Avoid standalone projects and tightly couple AI initiatives with the overall corporate strategy.

- **Dual-Pronged Engagement**
  - **Top-Down:** Leadership connects overarching business priorities to specific "AI domains" (focus areas like a department, core product, or contact center).
  - **Bottom-Up:** Actively crowdsource ideas and roadblocks from teams on the ground (using hackathons, submission forms, or briefing sessions) to inform daily operational strategy.

## Step 2: Selecting use cases for Minimum Viable Product and early wins

Organizations must carefully evaluate and prioritize AI use cases based on business impact and implementation feasibility. A suggested framework is the ***value vs. feasibility matrix***, which helps identify initiatives that deliver meaningful outcomes while remaining practical to implement.

- **Business Value Evaluation:** Assess impact on customers/employees, alignment with top business objectives, and potential for reuse/extensibility.

- **Actionability & Feasibility Assessment:** Analyze required usability/accuracy, ease of adoption/integration, speed to value, technical fit, data readiness/trustworthiness, and risk tolerance. **Feasibility** focuses on whether the solution *can* technically and operationally be implemented. **Actionability** focuses on whether the solution *can realistically be adopted and used effectively* to drive decisions or outcomes.

## Step 3: Defining AI ethics and governance

An AI governance blueprint establishes the organization's guiding principles for responsible AI use at every level, including business processes, management, deployment, and continuous learning. It embeds risk mitigation directly into the corporate culture.

- **Scope and Objectives:** Clarify the ethics policy's objectives while defining the boundaries of AI applications, tracking the shifting regulatory landscape, and aligning essential stakeholders.

- **AI Guiding Principles:** Establish clear ethical boundaries and active risk mitigation strategies covering usage limitations, cybersecurity, bias reduction, data protection, and continuous team training.

- **Governance Structure:** Implement a dedicated oversight body or committee to manage security, compliance, and accountability, fully integrating this structure into existing organizational processes.

- **Workforce Empowerment:** Equipping and training the workforce to safely, responsibly, and confidently utilize AI tools within approved boundaries.

## Step 4: Measure and track Progress

AI initiatives should include clear measurement frameworks from the design stage onward, with defined baselines and success criteria. Continuous monitoring ensures that projects deliver measurable operational and business outcomes.

- **Model Quality Metrics:** Monitor the accuracy, factuality, reliability, and security of the AI models.

- **System Metrics:** Ensure platforms run efficiently, reliably, and at scale (revealing bottlenecks and compute cost optimization).

- **Adoption Metrics:** Track how users interact with new tools (frequency of usage and qualitative feedback).

- **Operational Metrics:** Measure whether AI features deliver downstream returns, such as improved processes.

- **Business Impact Metrics:** Provide visibility into strategic financial goals (increasing revenue, reducing costs, mitigating risk, or accelerating innovation).
