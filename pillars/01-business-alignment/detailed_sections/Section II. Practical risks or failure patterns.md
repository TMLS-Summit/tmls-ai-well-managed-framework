# Section II. Practical risks or failure patterns

- ❌ **Technology-First Thinking:** Building AI systems because the technology is available, not because a validated business need exists. Initiatives lack a clear problem statement, measurable objective, or sponsoring business owner, resulting in solutions in search of a problem.

- ❌ **Orphaned Systems:** No accountable business owner is identified after initial deployment. Without a designated owner, models go unmonitored, data pipelines degrade, and the system drifts from its original business purpose with no mechanism for escalation or remediation.

- ❌ **Vanity Metrics:** Success is measured by activity (models deployed, data processed, features released) rather than business outcomes (revenue impact, cost reduction, risk mitigation). Teams appear productive while delivering no demonstrable value to the organization.

- ❌ **Scope Creep:** The original business case no longer reflects current reality as requirements expand without corresponding updates to feasibility, resourcing, or success criteria. Unchecked scope erodes ROI projections and delays value delivery, often masking a fundamentally misaligned initiative.

- ❌ **Zombie Systems:** Systems that continue to consume compute, maintenance, and operational resources long after they have ceased to deliver business value. Absent sunsetting criteria defined at inception, these systems persist indefinitely, crowding out investment in higher-value initiatives.

- ❌ **Misaligned Incentives:** Data science and engineering teams are evaluated on model performance benchmarks while business teams are measured on operational outcomes, creating a structural disconnect. Without shared success metrics, AI initiatives optimize for technical elegance rather than business impact.

- ❌ **Data Readiness Overconfidence:** Teams underestimate the quality, completeness, or accessibility gaps in their data assets and proceed to model development before readiness is confirmed. Discovered late in the lifecycle, these gaps cause costly rework, missed timelines, and eroded stakeholder confidence.

- ❌ **Governance as an Afterthought:** Ethics, compliance, and risk controls are applied reactively after deployment rather than embedded from the design stage. This exposes the organization to regulatory penalties, reputational damage, and model failures that could have been identified and mitigated earlier in the lifecycle.

- ❌ **Pilot Purgatory:** Initiatives that demonstrate early promise but never transition to production at scale. Often caused by unclear ownership, insufficient change management, or failure to define actionability criteria upfront, leaving high-potential use cases perpetually “in evaluation” without delivering business value.

- ❌ **Isolated AI Projects:** AI initiatives developed in silos, disconnected from broader corporate strategy, existing data infrastructure, or adjacent business processes. Without integration into the organization’s operating model, outputs cannot be acted upon, and learnings are not shared, duplicating effort and compounding costs.
